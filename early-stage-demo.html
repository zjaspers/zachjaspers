Early-Stage Demo System

A system that turns a horizontal enterprise platform into a small set of buyer-specific, reusable first-demo stories. It helps Solution Consultants choose the right story, run a focused 30-minute flow, and adapt with discovery without rebuilding from scratch.

Explore the operating model ↓See the three plays ↓

Originally built as an internal system for a horizontal enterprise platform. This public case study generalizes the methodology and sanitizes the examples; customer identities, internal scripts, and company-specific product details are omitted.

What it is

“Who am I meeting with, and what demo should I run?”

The seller-facing webpage puts a demo methodology into a usable workflow. A Solution Consultant (SC) selects a buyer-aligned play and gets an ordered run sheet: what to demonstrate, why it matters, and enough talk track to carry the story.

Ready for the meeting

Standard intro decks frame the conversation. The run sheet carries that framing into the demonstration.

Depth when it is useful

Proof points, transitions, and discovery branches sit beneath the main steps so the delivery flow stays readable.

The problem

Platform breadth makes the first story harder to choose.

A horizontal platform can support many workflows and buyers. That breadth creates a recurring presales problem: the first demo becomes a tour of everything the product can do, or each SC has to construct a narrative from scratch.

I worked backward from patterns in actual demos to identify reusable operating stories. The starting point was the buyer’s responsibility and the belief the demo needed to establish. Capabilities earned their place by helping prove that story.

The core operating model

Start with the buyer. Follow the work.

Buyer → Standard Play → Standard Story → Discovery Branches

  1. 01

    Buyer

    Identify who owns the problem, what they are accountable for, and where their operating story begins.

  2. 02

    Standard Play

    Select the repeatable business problem and the belief the demonstration needs to establish.

  3. 03

    Standard Story

    Follow a coherent sequence of work, handoffs, and visible proof through an ordered run sheet.

  4. 04

    Discovery Branches

    Use what has been learned to adapt the scenario or add the relevant depth while preserving the core story.

Three generic play archetypes

Different buyers. Different entry points.

Operations

For the person responsible for day-to-day execution.

Start → Route → Execute → Verify → Manage → Measure

Show how a priority becomes assigned work, how completion is verified, and how leaders handle exceptions and see results.

Belief to establish: operational priorities can become visible, verified execution with less manual chasing.

Communications

For the person responsible for getting the right message to the right audience.

Plan → Target → Publish → Experience → Act → Measure

Begin with the publisher’s intent, follow the recipient’s experience, and connect communication to action and feedback.

Belief to establish: communication can be managed through the response it creates, beyond the act of publishing.

Initiative Execution

For the person accountable for a coordinated program or change.

Plan → Coordinate → Target → Launch → Execute → Monitor → Measure

Start upstream with the initiative owner, then follow how a plan reaches the people doing the work and how progress returns to the owner.

Belief to establish: a distributed initiative can retain coordination, accountability, and visibility as it moves into execution.

The 30-minute first demo

Earn the next conversation.

The first demo has a deliberately limited job: help the buyer recognize their problem, understand a credible path to an outcome, and identify what deserves deeper validation. A focused 30-minute flow provides a repeatable starting point.

The story follows work as it changes hands. Personas change when the process requires a handoff. Each moment answers a business question and leaves room for the buyer to test the story against their own reality.

A useful constraint

Every step needs a reason to be there.

If a capability does not advance the buyer’s core story or answer a discovery-backed question, save it for a later conversation.

CORE vs BRANCH

Keep the spine stable. Adapt with purpose.

Core

The repeatable story

The essential sequence that establishes the play’s central belief: something requires action, work reaches an owner, execution produces proof, and the accountable leader can respond. This is the stable 30-minute spine.

Branch

The discovery-led depth

A relevant variation or extension activated by a buyer’s operating context. Discovery can change the trigger, constraints, or proof requirement without forcing a new demo architecture.

Illustrative example

Same execution story. Different operating conditions.

An Operations play might begin with a routine check or an urgent exception. If discovery reveals that readiness affects who can perform the work, the SC can explore that branch, then return to execution and verification. The buyer’s context changes what needs emphasis.

The reusable authoring unit

Make each story moment coachable.

Business Question → Setup → Show → Proof → Value Message → Transition

This structure sits behind the scripts. Authors use it to build consistent moments; SCs see a usable run sheet without having to consciously work through the framework during delivery.

  1. 01

    Business Question

    What does the buyer need answered at this point in the story?

  2. 02

    Setup

    Establish the situation, actor, and reason the next action matters.

  3. 03

    Show

    Demonstrate the specific action or handoff that answers the question.

  4. 04

    Proof

    Make the result observable so the audience can see what changed.

  5. 05

    Value Message

    Connect that evidence to the buyer’s operating outcome.

  6. 06

    Transition

    Explain why the next moment follows from this one.

How discovery feeds the system

Give discovery somewhere to go.

Discovery supplies the operating context: the desired outcome, the trigger for work, its owner, the handoffs, the exceptions, and the evidence of success. The SC uses that context to select a play, adjust the scenario, and choose branches worth exploring.

Unknowns remain questions to validate. A standard story provides a starting hypothesis; the buyer’s answers determine where it needs to change.

Why this matters

Scale the judgment behind a good demo.

The work captures decisions that often stay in an experienced SC’s head: where to begin, what to prove, when to change perspective, and which details can wait. Those decisions become reusable material for preparation, coaching, and refinement.

The intended leverage is a team that can start from shared story architecture and spend its judgment on the buyer’s actual differences. Leaders can review the reasoning behind a demo, and authors can improve a play or story moment for future use.

The contribution is the operating model connecting buyer context, narrative, and proof. The webpage makes that model practical to use.

Design principles

What the system should preserve.

Buyer before breadth

Begin with accountability and the outcome the buyer needs to believe is possible.

Follow the work

Let actions, handoffs, and evidence determine the sequence and persona changes.

Standardize with room for judgment

Keep a reusable core, earn each branch through discovery, and make useful learning available to the next SC.
