Fieldnote — Discovery & Deal Strategy

An opportunity workspace for moving from rough enterprise discovery to a structured operating model, then turning that evidence into an explicit buying thesis, proof plan, and next move.

The problem

Discovery is useful only if it changes what you do next.

Enterprise discovery often lives as chronological call notes. Important context is present, but it is hard to see what is actually known, what is assumed, where the operating gaps are, or what should change in the opportunity strategy.

What breaks

Teams can complete discovery without reaching a clear point of view. Notes accumulate, but the underlying problem, required buyer belief, proof sequence, and next best move remain implicit.

What I wanted instead

One opportunity workspace where the same customer + initiative can move from Discovery to Deal Thesis without recreating context or separating evidence from judgment.

The opportunity workspace

One customer. Two levels of understanding.

01

Discovery

Capture how the customer actually operates: outcome, trigger, ownership, flow, decisions, variance, coordination, exceptions, proof, visibility, facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.

02

Deal Thesis

Interpret the evidence: underlying problem, business stakes, buying actors, required buyer belief, structural advantage, proof sequence, risks, exclusions, and next best move.

03

Carry it forward

Export a concise strategy brief or copy demo context downstream so the demo, pilot, workshop, or executive conversation reflects the actual buying thesis.

Deal Thesis

Turn discovery into a point of view.

The Deal Thesis tab sits inside the same customer + initiative workspace. It does not replace discovery or manufacture strategy. It forces the team to make the commercial interpretation explicit.

Underlying problem

What is actually broken beneath the stated requirements, and why does it matter now?

Business stakes

What operational, economic, strategic, or risk impact makes the problem worth changing?

Buying actors

Who owns, shapes, approves, or can block the decision—and what does each need to believe?

Required buyer belief

What must become true in the customer’s mind for the opportunity to advance?

Structural advantage

Why are we unusually well suited to solve this particular operating problem?

Proof sequence

What must be demonstrated, validated, referenced, or quantified—and in what order?

Risks & unknowns

What could materially change the thesis or prevent progress?

Intentional exclusions

What are we deliberately leaving out because it does not improve the current decision?

Next best move

What should happen next, why, and what needs to become true afterward?

Demo strategy context

A concise handoff that carries the thesis into the Demo Script Standardizer without rebuilding the opportunity context.

What it standardizes

Structure, not judgment.

Evidence discipline

Facts, hypotheses, unknowns, and source notes stay distinct so uncertainty remains visible instead of disappearing inside a polished story.

Strategic discipline

The thesis makes the underlying problem, buyer belief, differentiation, proof plan, risks, and deliberate exclusions explicit—without pretending the tool can make the judgment for the seller.

Outputs

Discovery and strategy that can travel.

Output 01

Structured discovery record

A durable view of the customer’s operating reality and the evidence behind it.

Output 02

Deal Thesis brief

A concise commercial interpretation: what the customer is actually buying, what must be proven, what could break, and what should happen next.

Output 03

Demo strategy context

A reusable handoff into the downstream demo workflow so the presentation is built around the buying thesis rather than a feature inventory.

Design principles

What the system is meant to preserve.

Discovery is iterative

A call ending does not mean understanding is complete.

Reasoning should be traceable

The strategy should remain connected to the evidence that created it.

Judgment stays human

The system structures the thinking; it does not invent certainty or replace the seller’s point of view.
